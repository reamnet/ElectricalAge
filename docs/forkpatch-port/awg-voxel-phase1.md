# Copper 12 AWG 600 V voxel cable feasibility

Reviewed on `feature/awg-voxel-cables` at `125a5dc14`. Voxel placement for this wire is deferred until the electrical network and material ledger can represent it together. The current six-node wire is usable as before.

## Source contract

`eln:six_node@2200` maps to `eln:copper_12_awg_cable_600v`. `UtilityCables.kt` and `UtilityCableDescriptor.kt` define a single copper conductor of 3.309 mm², 20 A nominal, 600 V insulation and a 105 °C jacket transition. `WirePhysics.kt` uses 0.017241 Ω·mm²/m at 20 °C and 0.00393/K, giving approximately 5.210 mΩ/m at 20 °C. `WireThermalPhysics.kt` models length-dependent copper mass, jacket cooling and fusion. The original current rating is nominal. `UtilityCableElement.kt` heats from actual electrical losses and uses the jacket and copper failure transitions instead of a separate instantaneous 20 A breaker. Insulation exposure can latch a 100 Ω ground fault. The present six-node placement spends 1 m and breaking it returns one metre's worth of material as wire scrap. Grid spans already support fractional meter spools and a different recovery path.

The graph and ledger are the minimum coupled change. Wiring up only `SixNodeItem` and a tier/profile would place a cable with misleading ratings, branch losses and refunds. A controlled first implementation should land the AWG edge model and paid-length checks with no player entry point, then enable the item mode after end-to-end tests pass.

## Acceptance sequence

1. Check descriptor 2200 and its registered key, original one-metre placement, and reload of six-node blocks with damage and heat. Check profile round trips through NBT and the voxel publish codec.
2. Compare straight, bent and unequal tee routes with known loads. Check segment resistance at 20 °C and at elevated temperature, conserved currents and per-edge heat. Check cross-block and device connections, overvoltage exposure and the ground fault, jacket damage under overload, and copper fusion.
3. In survival, verify insufficient and rejected placement leave the exact original stack and world. For corners, suspended runs, repeated cut/reconnect/removal and reload, verify recovered plus still-placed paid length equals charged length. Include creative placement, failed cuts and fractional remainder. Check collision and render continuity using the same routed sizes.
4. Run focused mechanics and recipe-independent regressions and data generation twice. Preserve historical snapshots.

After this passes for descriptor 2200, generalize by reading material, area, ampacity, insulation, jacket limit and render data from each existing single-conductor descriptor. Add copper and aluminum profiles in groups with parameterized physics, save and conservation tests. Multicore routing needs isolated edge loads and separate pin/fault handling and is outside this phase.

## Baseline checks

The focused run passed 26 existing wire physics, wire thermal and grid cable planning unit tests. All nine selected GameTests passed, covering the existing spool and six-node placement, junction heating, insulation failure, jacket and fusion transitions, hot grid span reload and refund, routing to an elevated machine, and harness smoke coverage. Both data-generation runs wrote zero files. These checks establish the existing behavior. AWG voxel placement and rendering require the extensions above before they can be tested.

## Phase 1A electrical foundation

`AwgCableEdge` stamps one resistor per physical run between explicit MNA voltage states. A verified joint shares one state among all incident edges, including when the runs lie in different blocks. Separate terminals stay isolated. The future voxel topology owner will resolve joints, register shared states once, and detach edges before releasing their endpoints. The edge accepts a positive conductor length in meters and a temperature in Celsius. It uses the existing material and cross-sectional resistance model, and it exposes voltage drop, signed current and dissipated power through the standard resistor API.

Phase 1A exercises this contract at the circuit level. World joint discovery, endpoint lifetime across chunk boundaries, thermal and failure processes, and all player-facing behavior remain later integration steps.

## Phase 1B material accounting foundation

`AwgWireLength` records the descriptor ID, the pre-render centerline length in whole route voxels, and whether the run was paid. One Minecraft block of routed length represents one meter, as in AWG six-node placement. Sixteen route voxels therefore equal one meter, and an individual voxel costs exactly 0.0625 m. The stored physical length can feed `AwgCableEdge` directly. Creative wire retains physical length while carrying no refund. A paid and a creative run can share an electrical endpoint while retaining separate material ledgers.

The adapter debits the original spool's meter balance using the descriptor's read/write routines. It requires an exact, representable debit, keeps all positive fractional remainders and ignores the six-node consumption epsilon. Splits transfer integer units from the original ledger. Joins consume the donor ledger and require matching descriptor and payment provenance. An intact refund drains the selected ledger once and creates a spool of the original wire type for that length. The persisted ledger requires the expected descriptor and strict integer fields. The loader rejects invalid lengths instead of deriving a refundable balance from geometry.

The future placement transaction must partition accepted route steps across edges before producing render pieces. A bend charges both route legs, with no charge for a corner marker, render overlap, junction body or sag. A tap transfers the original trunk's paid length to the split edges and charges only the new branch. The world owner must replace old ledgers on reload, install each new ledger once, and commit spool debits and world changes together. Phase 1B has no registration, router, rendering, or world-save integration.

## Phase 1C electrothermal run foundation

`AwgCableRun` owns a paid-length ledger and couples one `AwgCableEdge` to the source's `WireThermalPhysics` and `WireThermalLoad`. It samples committed resistor and fault power on the existing electrical process clock, then updates heat, cooling, and failure on the simulator's slow clock. Ambient exchange uses the source cooling conductance frozen for each step and an exponential temperature update. It adds no series resistance to stabilize short runs. Endpoint thermal conduction between runs is reserved for later world integration. Resistance restamping uses the existing span refresh tolerance.

The source voltage-exposure curve latches a 100 Ω ground fault. The six-node wire has a center load, while the voxel run has two ends. This implementation latches the fault at the higher-stress end and retains that end on reload. Heating can destroy the jacket and remove the fault resistor, while preserving the damage record. Full copper fusion permanently opens the run. A damaged run yields only paid metal as scrap. A fused run or creative material yields no refund.

The versioned run payload records joules relative to 20 °C, jacket and fusion state, insulation exposure, fault endpoint, and the length ledger. Voltage states are supplied by the topology owner and solved again after attaching. A split partitions energy by length, retains damage on both portions, and keeps one fault at its original endpoint. A join conserves energy and uses the greater exposure. Faulted or incompatible runs keep separate ledgers and thermal states. Placement, voxel-part serialization, chunk and joint lifecycle, and world-facing failure visuals remain future work.

## Phase 1D-A controlled world placement

`AwgCablePart.place` lays one axis-aligned floor run in a loaded block using the existing voxel collision checks. It stages the meter debit on a copy of the existing descriptor 2200 spool and applies it only after placement succeeds. The part owns one versioned run payload and a separate visual shape. A level-scoped pool shares a voltage state at coincident route endpoints and releases it after the last incident run detaches. Each run remains an independent resistor and thermal mass. The normal legacy floor-edge connection exposes the midpoint terminal to compatible adjacent six-node devices. A one-microohm terminal contribution applies at device contacts only.

The world GameTest places two paid runs between the native Electrical Source and Power Resistor, measures series current and voltage drop, reloads one voxel node from its saved tag, then removes that run and checks the exact refund and open circuit. It also exercises the existing link-consistency audit. The reload test covers the actual node unload/reconstruction path. Ordinary chunk eviction retains ELN nodes in the simulation under the existing policy, and this phase has no dedicated chunk-eviction or server-restart test.

Controlled placement currently supports straight floor segments only. Voxel routing, other mounting faces and machine pin layouts, a dedicated in-world unequal-branch and fusion fixture, client visual checks and fault visuals remain later work. Phase 1C's numerical branch and fusion tests still cover the underlying run model.

## Phase 1D-B1 player controls

Hold Copper 12 AWG Cable 600V in the main hand. Face along the desired axis, then sneak and right-click the top of a solid floor block to lay one centered meter in the block above. Ordinary right-click still uses six-node placement. To remove a voxel run, aim at it and mine it with shears, as with the other voxel cables.

Only the server commits placement and the spool debit. It checks the hand, player permission and reach before calling the controlled placement operation. Rejected placements leave the original spool untouched. Survival removal drops the paid amount for intact wire, and creative placement uses a non-refundable ledger. Each click lays one block, with no multi-block routing or corner planning. The straight run uses the existing insulated voxel mesh and sprite.

## Phase 1D-B2 floor routing

With the spool in the main hand, sneak-right-click in air to enter route mode. Sneak-right-click the tops of the first and last floor blocks at the same elevation. Both endpoint blocks are included. Each must be within reach when clicked. Walking between clicks allows longer runs, up to 64 blocks. Sneak-right-click in air again to cancel. A plan expires after a minute, and completion returns the spool to one-block placement. Ordinary right-click retains six-node behavior.

The route is divided at voxel-block boundaries by the existing cable router’s piece builder. Each piece must have a loaded, supported, permitted destination, and the existing voxel placement checks test collision before laying. The spool is debited on a copy, the paid length is partitioned among the parts, and the original spool is updated only after the entire route succeeds.

Aligned endpoints lay a straight route. Diagonal endpoints try a single floor bend along X first, then along Z first if the first candidate is refused. The two elbow arms share a voltage state and each owns its own length and heat. Both legs are charged along the original centerline. Shared elbow geometry adds no material cost. These are bounded floor candidates. The spool has no route preview.

Moving between mounting faces needs a separate extension. Generalize the saved AWG part to carry its attachment face and a three-axis centerline, add explicit confirmed joints at bends and block edges, and connect zero-length corner geometry without creating refundable wire or a simulated resistor. Feed supported-face routes from the existing surface search into that model and allocate length before the piece builder adds corner fill or stubs. Then extend pin matching and test corner continuity, overlap, rollback, and paid-length recovery.
